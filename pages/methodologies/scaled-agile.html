Scaled Agile — SAFe, LeSS & Friends

Agile was designed for one small team. Once you have fifteen teams on one product, the easy answers stop. The scaled frameworks — SAFe, LeSS, Nexus, Scrum@Scale, the Spotify model — each take a different opinion on how to coordinate without strangling the teams underneath.

SAFe at a Glance

The Most-Adopted (and Most-Critiqued)

  • Agile Release Train (ART) — 50–125 people aligned to one cadence.
  • PI Planning — every 10–12 weeks, all teams in the train plan together for two days.
  • Roles — Release Train Engineer, Product Manager, System Architect, Business Owners.
  • Levels — Team, Program (Essential SAFe), Large Solution, Portfolio.
  • Critique — too prescriptive, too much process for process's sake; the original Agile signatories largely don't endorse it.
Tradeoffs

What to Watch Out For

  • Scaling adds coordination cost. Sometimes the answer is fewer teams, not a bigger framework.
  • "Two-pizza team" rule of thumb — if a team needs more than two pizzas to feed, it should split.
  • Conway's Law wins. Your architecture will look like your org chart; design accordingly.
  • Framework purity matters less than clear shared cadences and minimal handoff friction.
  • Spotify model isn't a recipe — it's a snapshot of one company at one point. Don't copy it; understand what it solved.
  • Big upfront training spend for SAFe certifications — Scaled Agile Inc. is a thriving business in its own right.
